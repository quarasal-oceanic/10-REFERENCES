## Overview

This section documents human physiology, psychology, ergonomics, and environmental requirements associated with long-term underwater habitation.

The objective is to understand how underwater environments affect human health, performance, comfort, and social interaction.

## 1. Core Research Areas

### 1.1 Human Physiology

- Pressure-related physiological effects.
- Respiratory requirements.
- Oxygen and carbon dioxide exposure.
- Thermal regulation.
- Circadian rhythm.
- Long-duration environmental adaptation.

### 1.2 Environmental Psychology

- Isolation and confinement.
- Stress and cognitive performance.
- Lighting and sleep cycles.
- Social interaction.
- Mental well-being.

### 1.3 Human Factors and Ergonomics

- Interior spatial design.
- Movement and circulation.
- Workspace ergonomics.
- Accessibility.
- Human-machine interaction.

### 1.4 Underwater Habitat Architecture

- Residential environments.
- Research and working spaces.
- Recreational facilities.
- Observation spaces.
- Lighting and acoustic design.

### 1.5 Human Safety

- Emergency evacuation.
- Medical facilities.
- Fire protection.
- Emergency communication.
- Crew training.

## 3. Research Questions

1. What physiological limitations affect long-term human habitation underwater?
2. How does prolonged isolation affect psychological well-being?
3. What spatial configurations support comfort and operational efficiency?
4. How should natural and artificial lighting be designed for underwater living?
5. What medical and emergency facilities are essential?

## Central Research Question

**How can underwater living environments support human health, safety, psychological well-being, and long-term habitation?**
